## 1. The core finding that shapes everything

The three research passes converge on one thing, independently: **the roots that matter most for "daily life" and the roots that are most frequent in the Quran are almost the same list.** Of the daily-life priority roots (আসসালামু আলাইকুম, আলহামদুলিল্লাহ, ইলম, ঈমান, দোয়া, etc.), nearly all sit in the top third of the 84-root Quranic frequency table. That means one root list can serve both goals — this book doesn't need to choose between "Quranic" and "daily life," they're the same curriculum.

The second major finding, from the daily-life research, is a **real correction worth building into the book on purpose**: several words Bangladeshi families assume are Arabic are actually **Persian** (নামাজ, রোজা, ফেরেশতা, বেহেশত, দোজখ, গুনাহ, মেহমান, পাক). Using one of these as a "here's the Arabic root" example would be a factual error. Rather than just avoiding them, I want to make this a recurring "spot the difference" device in the story itself — see §3.

## 2. Story concept — working title **"মূল ও শাখা" (Root & Branch)**

Research (pedagogy pass, §1–§2) recommends: lead with a concrete, already-familiar word before revealing the abstract root; use a literal "family tree" metaphor; make the mind-map something the child *builds*, not just views; keep story, mind-map, and dictionary as **one visual system**, not three.

A garden/tree metaphor satisfies all of this at once, and gives the story, the UI mind-map, and the word index the same underlying image instead of three unrelated designs:

- Each **root** is a tree. The three root letters are its trunk.
- Each **derived word** is a fruit or branch growing from that trunk — same root-color every time it appears (in story text, in the tree diagram, in the index).
- A recurring elder character (a "নানা"-style figure, matching what already works in নূর দ্বীপ অভিযান) tends an old walled garden where every tree is a word-family, and teaches the child protagonist to recognize which fruits belong to which tree.
- The Persian-vs-Arabic distinction becomes a specific story beat: some plants in the garden *look* like they belong but were actually brought from a neighboring garden (Persian) generations ago — still loved and used, just not from these roots. This turns a potential embarrassing mistake into a memorable, honest teaching moment ("নামাজ গাছ থেকে আসেনি, কিন্তু আমরা এখনও এটা ভালোবাসি" type framing — to be written properly, not literal).
- Suggested protagonist: a new child character (not মাহদী বিন মামুন — a different book, own identity), possibly a cousin or neighbor in the same village world for light continuity if you want it, or fully separate. **Open question for you, see §7.**

This also gives a natural answer to "mind map": the tree diagram *is* the mind map, rendered the same way whether the child is inside the story, browsing the word index, or reviewing.

## 4. Word-formation patterns taught across the pilot

Rather than teaching grammar abstractly, the same 4 patterns recur across every pilot root (this is deliberate — per the pedagogy research, seeing one pattern-shape repeat across many roots is what actually builds intuition):

| Pattern | Role | Example (ع-ل-م) | Example (ر-ح-م) |
|---|---|---|---|
| فَاعِل | "the one who does it" | عَالِم (a knower/scholar) | رَاحِم / رَحِيم (merciful one) |
| فِعْل / مصدر | the action/concept itself | عِلْم (knowledge) | رَحْمَة (mercy) |
| يَفْعَلُ | "he does it" (present) | يَعْلَمُ (he knows) | يَرْحَمُ (he shows mercy) |
| مَفْعَل / مَفْعُول | place or object of the action | مَعْلُوم (known [thing]) | مَرْحُوم (one shown mercy) |

In the garden metaphor, each pattern becomes the same "shape of fruit" regardless of which tree it grows on — a فَاعِل-word is always drawn/colored the same way whether it's from ع-ل-م or ر-ح-م, so the child starts recognizing the *pattern* independent of the root, exactly as the pedagogy research recommends.

## 5. Class template (per your original spec)

Every class in this book has the four parts you asked for, mapped onto what the research supports:

1. **মূল গল্প (Main story)** — the day's root introduced through a concrete, already-story-familiar word first (not the bare root letters). The recurring elder character dramatizes the word family through action, per the pedagogy research's "character-driven over narrator-driven" finding — e.g., the child watches someone actually be رَحِيم (merciful) before being told the root.
2. **শব্দ গঠনের নিয়ম (Word-formation rule)** — one pattern from §4 shown as a "fruit shape," with 3–4 real words from that root poured into it. Root letters color-highlighted identically to how they'll appear in the mind-map and index.
3. **দৈনন্দিন জীবনে ব্যবহার (Daily-life / Bangla usage)** — the root's daily phrase (আলহামদুলিল্লাহ, ইকামত, etc.) and, where relevant, the Persian-vs-Arabic correction from §2.
4. **ঐতিহাসিক গল্প (History story)** — a real, sourced moment tied to the root: e.g., ص-ب-ر (patience, second batch) → the story of Prophet Ayyub; ر-ح-م → a moment of mercy from seerah. **These need to be selected and verified per-root with the same rigor as the Quranic references — not yet done, flagged as remaining work.**

Each class ends with a light retrieval task (per pedagogy research — production beats passive reading): e.g., "which fruit belongs on this tree?" matching a word to its root before moving on.

## 6. Word index & mind map (site structure)

Directly reusing the "three-view, one-data-model" principle from the pedagogy research, and নূর দ্বীপ অভিযান's own already-working patterns (word cross-linking, badge wall, threads):

- **Root hub page** (one per root) — the tree visual, all derived words as fruit, each fruit linking to: (a) the class where it was introduced, (b) its sibling words on the same tree, (c) the word-formation pattern it belongs to.
- **Word index** — browsable by root (primary, Hans-Wehr-style clustering per the research) or alphabetically/by class (secondary) — two entry points into the same data.
- **Mind map** — the same tree rendering, zoomed out to show all learned roots at once, thematically clustered by island (see §1 of the research doc: Allah's Attributes, Worship, Family, Nature, etc.). This can literally be the book's own version of নূর দ্বীপ's island map — same UI convention, different islands.
- Every root gets one consistent accent color, used identically in story text, tree diagram, and index — this consistency is what the research flags as the actual retention mechanism, not any single clever diagram.
